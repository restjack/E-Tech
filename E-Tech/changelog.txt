  Features:
    - Factory export filter: a new device built INSIDE a Factorissimo factory
      that says what may leave it. This replaces the per-factory filter list
      that briefly lived on the outlet's Factories tab in 0.26.0. The rule

  Features:

      mod simply shows "Any". Applies on top of the item filters rather than
      instead of them, and rides along in blueprints, clones and copy-paste with
      the rest of the device's settings.

Version: 0.25.0
Date: 2026-08-09
  Bugfixes:
    - Factory outlet: it drained the interior end of the factory's own chest
      connections, fighting Factorissimo for the same chest. The pairing that
      breaks is the ordinary one - a REQUESTER outside feeding a PASSIVE
      PROVIDER inside, which is how ore gets from your miners into a factory.
      Factorissimo reads requester-outside as an input and runs the connection
      inwards; the outlet, reading logistic modes alone, saw a provider full of
      ore and pulled it straight back out, where the requester asked for it
      again. The ore never reached the machines and both mods were behaving
      exactly as designed. Interior connection chests are now left alone.
      Crucially it skips the CHEST, not the item and not the factory, so
      everything else inside still exports normally - the ore arriving through a
      connection stays put while the plates made from it leave. There is an
      opt-in if you deliberately want the outlet to drain one.
      Connections are read with a fresh lookup rather than from the cached
      factory table: those tables are remote snapshots up to five minutes old,
      so a connection built since the last scan was invisible and the chest
      looked like an ordinary provider.
  Features:
    - Factory outlet: "Serve the factories from their own stock first". This is
      the root fix for the loop the guards in 0.23.0 only blunt, and it is the
      one to try first. The full circle was: an interior provider chest holds an
      item, an interior requester chest wants it, the inlet auto-requests it from
      the outside network, the outlet reads that as demand and pulls the item out
      of the very factory that needs it, robots fly it across the base to the
      inlet, and the inlet pushes it back inside. Six steps to move an item
      between two chests on the same surface. The loop guards stop that by
      refusing to do step four, which ends the circling but leaves the interior
      requester unfed. With this on, the transfer happens directly inside
      instead: interior requester and buffer chests are filled from the interior
      provider chests before anything is pulled out at all. The deficit is gone,
      so the inlet has nothing to auto-request and the outlet sees no demand -
      there is no circle left to break. No robots are involved, so it works in a
      factory with no interior roboport. Shared out proportionally when supply is
      short, same as the inlet does, and moved stack by stack so spoilage and
      quality survive. The outlet's rate line reports what moved inside
      separately from what it pulled out, since those items never crossed the
      wall.

Version: 0.23.0
Date: 2026-08-09
  Features:
    - Factory outlet and inlet: the filter slots are quality-aware. Each slot
      is now an item-with-quality pick, and a new "Match quality on filters"
      checkbox under the slots decides what that quality means. Off (the
      default, and how filters have always behaved) a slot covers its item at
      every quality. On, a slot matches only the quality it was picked at, so
      an outlet can pull legendary iron plates and leave the normal ones inside
      - or blacklist exactly the normal ones. Slots picked before this version
      carry no quality at all and keep matching every quality until re-picked,
      so no existing filter changes meaning on upgrade. Those slots do render
      with the normal-quality badge, because the button has no way to draw
      "any" - they carry a tooltip saying so, since otherwise a filter set up
      before this version reads as "normal only" the moment the checkbox is
      ticked while still excluding every quality.
    - Factory outlet: four loop guards, each its own checkbox, all off by
      default. They all address one shape: an inlet with auto-request on is a
      requester in the same logistic network the outlet provides to. The inlet
      asks the network for what the factories are short of, the outlet reads
      that as demand and pulls those same items straight back out of those same
      factories, bots fly them to the inlet, the inlet pushes them inside, and
      round it goes. Nothing that wasn't already satisfiable gets satisfied -
      it just burns bots and CPU forever.
      "Ignore factory inlet auto-requests" - the demand scan skips the managed
      etech-inlet-auto section, so the round trip never starts; an inlet's own
      manual requests still count as demand. "Never pull what an inlet is asking
      for" - narrower: any item an inlet on this surface is currently
      auto-requesting is off limits to the outlet, even for genuine outside
      demand, while every other item still moves between factories normally.
      "Re-pull cooldown (10 s)" - an item the outlet just handed back can't be
      pulled again for 10 seconds, which is the one for demand that flickers on
      and off between passes and had the same stack teleporting out and back
      every 2 seconds indefinitely. "No storage re-pull (60 s)" - only bites
      with "Also pull from storage chests" on: an item the outlet returned into
      an interior storage chest stays there for 60 seconds instead of being
      pulled straight back out, and provider chests are untouched by it.
      The toggles ride along in blueprints and clones with the rest of the
      device's settings.
    - Factory fluid outlet: a loop guard of its own, and the fluid pair needed
      one more than the item pair did - fluid moves in bulk with no robots to
      throttle it. Pipe a fluid outlet and a fluid inlet onto the same header
      (one header per fluid being the normal way to plumb a base) and the outlet
      drains the factories' tanks, the fluid runs down the header into the
      inlet, and the inlet pushes it straight back into those same tanks.
      Nothing ever leaves and both devices work forever. With the guard on, the
      outlet stops pulling while any fluid inlet on its surface is holding the
      fluid it is picked for, and says so in its panel. Changing the pick still
      flushes the old fluid back normally - a guarded outlet must never end up
      holding a fluid it cannot shed.
    - Factory sensor: filter slots, the same ones the outlet and inlet have,
      plus quality matching. It broadcast every item it could see until now,
      truncated to the top 1000 by count, which on a real base is a wall of
      signals nobody wired anything to.
    - Factory sensor: an option to also count the interior storage chests, and
      an option to subtract what the factories are SHORT of. With the second
      one on, a signal reads positive when the factories have that item spare
      and negative when their requester chests are short by that much, so one
      wire carries both halves. Truncation now keeps the largest signals by
      magnitude rather than by value, or the most negative ones - the
      interesting ones - would be the first thing dropped.
    - Factory outlet: "Set filters from circuit network". The connected red and
      green item signals become the filter list and the slots grey out; the
      whitelist/blacklist dropdown still applies to that list. This is the
      vanilla 2.0 requester-chest idiom, and it is the only way to change what
      an outlet pulls without a player standing in front of it.
  Bugfixes:
    - Factory outlet: a storage chest's filter was matched by item name alone,
      so a chest filtered for legendary iron plate read as the right home for
      normal iron plate and quietly accepted it. Both halves of the filter are
      honoured now. A filter with no quality set still matches every quality.
    - Factory inlet: when it held less than the factories wanted in total, the
      first chest in its list took its whole deficit before the second was even
      looked at - so the factories at the end of the list got nothing, not
      less, pass after pass, indefinitely. The list order is stable, so the same
      factories starved every time. It now collects every chest's shortfall
      first and then divides what it has proportionally, with the units left
      over by the rounding walked out from a rotating start so the same chest
      does not collect the odd one every pass. When there is enough to go round,
      behaviour is exactly what it was.
    - Teleporter pads: the unpowered-pad alert pass rebuilt its icon table once
      per pad per player and re-read the connected-player list per pad. Both are
      built once now. The alert itself is still re-raised every pass on purpose -
      a custom alert that stops being re-added stops being shown.
  Optimizations:
    - The construction-ghost index is bounded. It kept one storage entry and one
      destroy registration per ghost, which is the right trade for a normal base
      and the wrong one for a ten-thousand-entity blueprint paste. Past 20 000
      ghosts on a surface it keeps updating the per-chunk demand totals but
      stops recording each ghost individually, and rebuilds itself from the
      world every 60 seconds until the paste drains. The cost is that demand
      reads slightly high while it is in that state - the outlet over-supplies
      and its own give-back pass returns the excess.

      used to invalidate every device's interior-chest cache, and any storage
      tank anywhere invalidated every device's factory list. On a base with bots
      continuously building that meant the caches were rebuilt every pass - the
      exact cost they exist to avoid. Both now trigger only for chests actually
      inside a factory and for real factory buildings.

      skipped after a single inventory read instead of walking every slot.

      longer in the background and no longer sags UPS while it runs.

      makes every device rebuild its factory list and its interior-chest cache

      re-validated at the moment it is used.
    - Factory outlet: the module-request scan is cut into six strips, one per

      multiple of the other.

  Features:
    - Factorissimo interior roboport: whole-floor logistics coverage, slots for

    - Robots could not charge inside factory buildings at all. Factorissimo runs
      the interior roboport on a void energy source, and robot charging is paid
      out of a roboport's stored energy - a void source stores none, so charging
      never begins no matter how many charging pads the roboport has or how much
      power each pad is given. Factorissimo's own hidden construction robots are
      defined with no energy drain and never charge, so the limitation only
      appears once player robots operate inside a factory.
    - "Charge bots from your power grid" (etech-factory-roboport-electric, on by
      default) replaces the void source with a real electric one, with a 200 MJ
      buffer and a 100 MW draw limit. This is what makes charging work. Charging
      inside a factory therefore draws from the same electric network as the rest
      of the base - roughly 1-2 MW per factory with a busy robot fleet, scaling
      with the number of robots in the air rather than with pad count. It uses the
      same low priority as a vanilla roboport, so an under-supplied grid slows
      charging instead of stopping machines. Turning the setting off restores
      Factorissimo's void source; robots then stop charging, and a warning is
      written to the log explaining why.
    - When the void source is kept, energy_usage and recharge_minimum are lowered
      from 50 kW / 40 MJ to 1 W / 1 W. A vanilla roboport satisfies a 40 MJ
      threshold from its 100 MJ buffer; a void source has no buffer and never can.
      This is a second, independent blocker on the same prototype.
    - The roboport's icon now matches the vanilla roboport. Factorissimo gives it a
      construction-chest icon, which goes unnoticed while the roboport is a hidden
      entity but appears as a chest consuming tens of MW in the electric network
      screen once it draws power.
    - Krastorio 2's roboport mode variants are patched to match. K2 builds its
      logistic-only and construction-only modes by deep-copying every roboport
      prototype during data-updates, before this feature runs, so the factory
      roboport's "-logistic-mode" and "-construction-mode" twins were copies of the
      stock version: void energy source, 4 pads at 500 kW, logistics radius 3, no
      robot slots. Switching a factory roboport's mode would have reverted all of
      the above. The twins now inherit the patched energy source, charging and
      slots, with K2's derived radius formulas recomputed from the new values.
    - Both toggles default on, unlike every other E-Tech feature toggle: this
      feature replaces a standalone mod rather than adding new content, and its
      grid-power half is what makes charging work at all. Turning the main toggle
      off restores Factorissimo's stock interior roboport, which has a logistics
      radius of 2.
    - REMOVE THE OLD MOD: delete factorissimo-roboport-buff from your mods folder.
      Leaving it installed is harmless but pointless - E-Tech now covers everything it
      did, and its two settings just sit there doing nothing. E-Tech logs a reminder
      when it sees the mod still present.

      that modify the interior roboport later cannot overwrite the result.
